**Sentence 1:** "前往南投縣埔里鎮參加國道六號南投段開工動土典禮，並應邀致詞"

**Translation:** President Chen Shui-bian presided over a ceremony Wednesday in Puli township, central Nantou County to kick off construction of the Nantou section of the National Expressway No. 6.

**Question**: 

1. Should we separate "前往" (go to), "參加" (participate), and "致詞" (give a speech) to three different events or merge them into one event? The definition of event should be clarified. For example, in Davidson's event semantics, he assumed that "events are individuated according to their *causal relationships*: one event is identical to another just in case they have the same causes, and the same effects. Given the causal nature of the evet identification criterion, Davidsan is not interested in states or parts of events (cited from course slides of "Semantics", offered by Lucia Tovena). 

2. If these three actions are considered three different events, then their time span is different, as the time span of "致詞" (give a speech) belongs to "參加" (the participation of the kick-off), and the event "前往" (go to) happens before the participation and speech. Also, "前往" sounds continuous, potentially implying the process of going to a place rather than the punctual action of going. Also, as "致詞" (give a speech) will last for a while, should we consider it punctual or continous? 

**Sentence 2:** "「國道六號南投段」的工程將創造超過一萬個就業機會"

**Translation:** ...the completion of the project would be a boon, as it is expected to create more than 10,000 job opportunities for nearby townships and areas.

**Question**: 

1. Both events will happen in the future. There is causal relations between them as well as the implicit temporal information of "if 004 happens then 005 will probably happen". How should we make this information structured?

2. A trivial one about assigning the event_id: if in one text, the main entity of the event changes, for example, from the president to the project, should we name it president_id then project_id, or we just name all of them with the same prefix.
